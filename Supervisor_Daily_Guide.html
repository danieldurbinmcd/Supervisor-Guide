The 5 Non-Negotiable Daily Steps
Step 01
Labor Setup Complete
Labor is not a day-of activity. It is a system built in advance. If labor is wrong this week, the failure happened last week.
  • Reviewed next week's schedule with every GM (7+ days out)
  • Confirmed no unassigned shifts — every shift is filled
  • Variable hours align to VLH guidelines
  • Scheduled TPPH aligns to performance expectations
  • Sales projections are realistic and documented
  • Peak periods properly staffed — positions align to volume
Step 02
Evaluate Results
Start with outcomes. Then diagnose the system. Use Coach Q to leverage findings.
  • Reviewed Sales & Transactions (Projection vs Actual)
  • Reviewed Labor Results (Hours vs Plan, TPPH vs Target)
  • Reviewed Service KPIs by daypart & hour (KVS, OEPE, R2P, Delivery)
  • Reviewed Operations Metrics (deployment, breaks, shift mgmt)
Step 03
Diagnose the System
You cannot solve a problem you have not correctly identified. Find the problem to solve the problem. Diagnose in order: Labor → Operations → Service → Sales
  • Identified whether issue is a demand problem or execution problem
  • Traced root cause — what decision created this outcome?
  • Determined if this is a planning failure or execution failure
  • Identified where the system broke
Step 04
Focus the Organization (80/20)
Not all problems matter equally. Identify top 1–2 system problems and top 1–2 leadership gaps. You are not solving every problem — you are ensuring the right problems are owned.
  • Identified top 1–2 system problems across the patch
  • Classified each: complicated (known fix) or complex (requires exploration)?
  • Identified top 1–2 leadership gaps
  • Ensured the right problems are assigned to the right owners
Step 05
Develop the GM
You do not solve the problem. You develop the person who will. If the GM leaves without ownership, the problem remains yours.
  • Had a GM development conversation today
  • GM achieved clarity — they see the real problem
  • GM achieved ownership — they own the result
  • GM committed to an adjustment — what will they do differently?

Diagnosis Framework
Diagnosis Order — Non-Negotiable Click each layer to expand diagnostic questions
→ → →
Labor = Foundation  |  Operations = Execution  |  Service = Experience  |  Sales = Outcome
Labor Diagnostic Questions
Start here -- labor is the foundation of every other outcome
Was the schedule built 7+ days in advance?
Did scheduled hours align to VLH guidelines?
Was the scheduled TPPH realistic given sales projections?
Were all shifts filled -- no unassigned openings?
Were peak periods correctly staffed by position?
Did actual hours match the schedule, or did the GM deviate?
What planning decision created this outcome?
If labor is off, stop here. Fix the labor plan before diagnosing operations or service.
Operations Diagnostic Questions
Only diagnose operations if labor is confirmed correct
Was deployment executed per the schedule -- right positions, right times?
Were breaks taken on time and covered correctly?
Did the shift manager adjust deployment as volume changed?
Were crew positioned for peak demand windows?
Was there a clear shift manager running the floor?
Did the team know their roles and stay in position?
What execution decision during the shift created this result?
Operations problems are execution failures. Find the decision that broke the system.
Service Diagnostic Questions
Only diagnose service if labor and operations are confirmed correct
What was OEPE by daypart -- where did it break down?
What was KVS time -- was the kitchen keeping pace?
Were guests backing up at a specific channel (DT, kiosk, counter, delivery)?
Was the bottleneck in taking the order, making the food, or handing out?
Did delivery orders impact drive-thru or counter speed?
Were R2P targets met during peak hours?
Is this consistent across days or isolated to specific shifts?
Service problems are symptoms. If labor and operations are right and service is still slow, find the specific friction point in the guest journey.
Sales Diagnostic Questions
Sales is an outcome -- diagnose what drove it, not the number itself
Was the variance a demand problem (fewer guests) or a check problem (lower APC)?
Did transaction count drop, average check drop, or both?
Was there a local demand factor -- weather, event, competitor promotion?
Did slow service cause guests to leave or reduce orders?
Were suggestive selling and upselling executed consistently?
Was the sales projection realistic -- or was it set too high?
Is this a one-time miss or a multi-week trend?
If sales are down and labor, operations, and service are all correct -- this is a demand problem, not an ops fix. Strategy and traffic drivers are the lever.
Problem Classification Matrix
Best Case
High Demand + Strong Execution
Sales and transactions are up. Labor is efficient. Service is fast. This is a winning week — reinforce what the GM did to create it.
Dig Deeper
High Demand + Weak Execution
Traffic is there but OEPE, TPPH, or labor is off. Execution problem, not a market problem. Find the deployment or scheduling gap.
Monitor
Low Demand + Strong Execution
Market may be soft, but the team is executing. Labor and service are right. Demand problem — strategy and traffic drivers needed, not operations fixes.
Act Here
Low Demand + Weak Execution
Both demand and execution are failing. Labor is likely the root — poor planning creates poor execution which drives poor results. Start at the schedule.

The 4 Non-Negotiable Questions
Every GM Conversation
1. What happened?
Get the facts from the GM — not your version.
2. Why did it happen?
Listen for depth. Surface answers ("it was busy") are not acceptable.
3. What decision caused it?
Trace it back. What planning or execution choice created this result?
4. What will you change next week?
This is the ownership moment. No answer = no ownership.

Core Principles
Find the Problem
If you solve the wrong problem, nothing improves. The most important skill is not execution — it is diagnosis. You cannot solve a problem you have not correctly identified.
Multiply Ownership
Performance improves when leaders at every level take responsibility. Ownership is not centralized — it is multiplied. If the GM leaves without ownership, the problem remains yours.
System Connection
Last week’s labor plan creates today’s operations, which drives service, which determines sales. You are not reacting to results — you are auditing decisions already made.
3 Outcomes per Conversation
Clarity — Do they see the real problem?
Ownership — Do they own the result?
Adjustment — What will they do differently?
Quick Reference — Diagnosis Questions
✖ Weak Thinking — Never Accept
“Sales were down”
“Service was slow today”
“It was just a bad day”
“We were short-staffed”
✓ Strong Thinking — Require This
“What decision created this outcome?”
“Was this a planning or execution failure?”
“Where did the system break?”
“What will I change next week?”
